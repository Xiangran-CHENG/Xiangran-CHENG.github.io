Research

From physical principles to materials applications, enabled by scientific tools.

Two fundamental questions guide our research: how do materials form, and how does structure determine their properties? We apply these principles to the design and screening of materials for energy storage and conversion, while developing scientific agents that connect evidence, models and experiments across all three directions.

01 / PRINCIPLES OF MATERIAL FORMATION

Synthesis Mechanisms and Prediction

How do thermodynamic driving forces, kinetic barriers and mass transport govern material formation?

We investigate why particular pathways emerge as precursors transform through intermediate states into target or competing products. Combining physical models, synthesis records and in situ observations, we aim to connect formation mechanisms with predictions of which material forms, through which pathway and under which conditions.

  • Thermodynamic driving forcesAssess phase stability, reaction energetics and competition under synthesis conditions.
  • Kinetics and transportModel nucleation, reactions, diffusion and growth to resolve pathways and timescales.
  • Route and condition selectionRecommend precursors and processing conditions, then refine predictions using experimental feedback.

Ongoing Projects

  • Metal Nanocluster Design and Synthesis Planning

    Connecting literature evidence, chemical constraints and agent-assisted planning to propose nanocluster structures and synthesis routes for experimental evaluation.

  • Mesoporous Materials: Synthesis and Formation Mechanisms

    Learning from synthesis records to recommend preparation conditions and test how competing formation mechanisms shape mesoporous structures.

  • In Situ Reaction and Phase-Evolution Modeling

    Combining time-resolved observations with thermodynamic and kinetic models to infer intermediates and predict phase evolution, with sulfide solid electrolytes as a research system.

02 / PRINCIPLES OF MATERIAL FUNCTION

Structure–Property Relationships and Prediction

How do composition and structure determine material properties, and how can we predict their response under working conditions?

We connect atomic arrangements, defects, interfaces and microstructures with functional behavior. Physics-based and data-driven models help us explain these relationships and predict how material properties change across length scales and operating conditions.

  • Represent material statesDescribe composition, local structure, domains and microstructure at the scales relevant to function.
  • Connect mechanisms across scalesLink structural evolution to transport, electrochemical response and mechanical behavior.
  • Predict and evaluate propertiesTest predictions against calculations and measurements, including uncertainty and transfer to new conditions.

Ongoing Projects

  • Three-Dimensional Domain Structures and Functional Response

    Predicting ferroelectric domain structures using physical and data-driven models, and investigating how domain evolution relates to functional response.

  • Multiscale Structure–Property Modeling of Cathode Materials

    Connecting composition and microstructure with transport, deformation and fracture from atomic to particle and electrode scales.

  • In Situ Characterization of Dynamic Battery Interfaces

    Tracking interfacial reactions and structural changes in polymer-electrolyte and solid-state batteries to test how interface evolution affects transport and stability.

  • Battery Degradation and Lifetime Prediction

    Using cycling histories to forecast device degradation and lifetime, and investigating how operating conditions affect predictive accuracy and transfer across test conditions.

03 / MATERIALS APPLICATIONS

Energy Materials Design and High-Throughput Screening

Which materials can meet the combined performance, stability and synthesis requirements of energy storage and conversion?

We translate application needs into material design objectives and explore candidate compositions and structures. Drawing on synthesis and structure–property models, high-throughput workflows help prioritize candidates for synthesis and testing. Our ongoing projects focus on energy storage; extending these methods to energy conversion is a longer-term direction.

Ongoing Projects

  • High-Throughput Electrolyte Discovery

    Screening polymer and solid electrolytes using density functional theory (DFT), machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) and property models, with selected candidates taken forward for synthesis and characterization.

From Design to Validation

  • Define the design spaceSelect candidate chemistries and structures around transport, stability and interfacial requirements.
  • Screen and prioritizeCombine first-principles calculations and learned models to assess multiple properties and synthesis constraints.
  • Validate under working conditionsEvaluate selected materials and interfaces experimentally, then use performance and degradation data to improve selection.

04 / TOOLS FOR SCIENTIFIC RESEARCH

Agentic Scientific Research

How can scientific agents use evidence and tools to plan, carry out and improve a research workflow?

We develop agents that connect literature, physical models, computational tools and experimental feedback. This direction supports the other three by turning research questions into traceable sequences of evidence gathering, hypothesis testing and study selection, with researcher oversight.

  • Build an evidence baseExtract source-grounded information and connect literature, data and multimodal observations.
  • Plan and coordinate studiesForm testable hypotheses, select tools and organize computational or experimental steps.
  • Learn from feedbackCompare outcomes with expectations, revise explanations and choose the next informative study.

Ongoing Projects

  • Research Agents for Hypothesis Testing

    Connecting literature extraction, knowledge graphs and multimodal evidence with hypothesis formulation, alternative explanations and research planning.

  • Closed-Loop Experimental Planning

    Using feedback and active learning to select synthesis or characterization steps that improve materials or resolve uncertainty about formation mechanisms.

  • Integrated Laboratory Workflows

    Developing connections between synthesis equipment, characterization, data capture and analysis toward traceable, progressively more autonomous research workflows.
