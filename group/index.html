THE BIG PICTURE

From understanding matter
to making useful materials.

Science connects observation, explanation and prediction. Materials research adds a practical question: how can we create the structures and functions that society needs?

Materials science links synthesis and processing with composition and structure, material properties, and performance in use. Application needs guide the inverse question: what material should we make, and how can we make it?

OUR POSITION

AI for predictive synthesis science.

We focus on the connection between a desired material and the pathway that makes it form. At the intersection of AI, reaction science and experiments, we study how to make this connection predictable and useful for synthesis decisions.

Our mission and strategy ↓

OUR MISSION

Understand formation.
Guide synthesis.

We aim to predict and guide material formation through models that can be tested and improved experimentally.

Our focus is the path from precursors through evolving intermediates to target and competing phases. We combine AI with thermodynamics, kinetics and transport to model these transformations, then use experiments to test predictions and guide the next synthesis decision.

OUR RESEARCH STRATEGY

Observe formation. Test mechanisms. Guide synthesis.

  1. Observe formationInfer evolving material states from synthesis records and time-resolved, multimodal measurements.
  2. Test mechanismsCompare competing pathways using physical models and experiments that distinguish their predictions.
  3. Guide synthesisSelect routes and conditions, test the resulting material, and learn from the outcome.

What progress means: predictive accuracy under new conditions, reproducible target formation, and experimentally verified function.

THE SYNTHESIS GAP

Our chosen challenge: reliable material formation.

Many established materials already have reliable recipes. We focus on targets and conditions for which the formation pathway remains uncertain: which intermediates appear, which products compete, and which controls favor the intended outcome. Thermodynamic stability is one constraint; kinetics, transport and processing history also shape what forms.
