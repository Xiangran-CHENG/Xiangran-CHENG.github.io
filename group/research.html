RESEARCH

Materials, synthesis and autonomous experimentation

Our research connects the formation of materials with the tools needed to study and control it.

Reaction pathways and synthesizability

Which phases appear during synthesis, and how can a process be steered toward a desired material? We study precursor conversion, transient intermediates and competing pathways, combining reaction data with thermodynamic and kinetic constraints.

We aim to connect literature mining, in-situ characterization and models of phase evolution to predict the effects of changes in temperature, time and precursor choice. Experimental validation is central to this effort.

Synthesis planning · Phase evolution · Physical constraints

Battery materials and interfaces

Our materials research builds on Xiangran Cheng's experience with polymer electrolytes, electrode–electrolyte interfaces and flexible batteries. These systems connect questions about chemistry and transport to the performance of working devices.

Experience in battery manufacturing also informs our approach: a material must be considered together with its processing route, integration into a device and reproducibility.

Polymer electrolytes · Interfacial chemistry · Flexible batteries

Autonomous experimentation

We are working toward an autonomous materials laboratory that connects synthesis planning with robotic experiments and multimodal analysis. The aim is to make each experiment useful for both evaluating a material and improving our understanding of its formation.

Our long-term vision links materials-space exploration, synthesis-route generation and experimental feedback, using active learning to decide which experiment will be most informative next.

Robotics · Multimodal observations · Active learning
